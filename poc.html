Executive Summary

From ten pilots to one governed program

Brampton has moved faster than most Canadian cities: a Council-approved AI Proof of Concept program, vendors selected across ten service areas, and real momentum. The risk now is the one that catches every fast mover — scaling capability faster than trust. This blueprint recommends one flagship move (a governed "Trusted 311" agent wrapped in Canada's first municipal Public AI Register), a sequenced portfolio behind it, and a benefits-realization discipline that reports value to Council in dollars, hours, and resident outcomes. Built entirely from the public record; every figure is adjustable to the City's actuals.

Technical

12 years in the Salesforce ecosystem at the front edge of enterprise AI — primary Data360 field seller, pre-release evaluator of generative and agentic AI, documented outcomes to 80× ROI.

Governed

AI delivered inside regulated environments — privacy governance across 20+ Loblaw lines of business, financial services compliance with PC Financial, healthcare and life sciences portfolios.

Community

Step 3 · The Recommendation

Govern first. Then scale the front door.

"Demos sell. Audits ship."Brampton already has the demos — ten PoCs and vendors selected. What no Canadian city has yet is the governed, transparent, benefits-measured version. That is the open lane.

Flagship: "Trusted 311" — a governed resident AI agent, wrapped in Canada's first municipal Public AI Register

One move that compounds three ways: it scales the PoC with the most resident value (311), it answers public scrutiny on costs, privacy, and fairness with radical transparency instead of press releases, and it makes Brampton the first city in Canada with a Helsinki-style public AI register — a national leadership position that costs less than one bad headline. NYC proves the downside of skipping this step; Bill 194 makes it the direction Ontario law is already heading.

First 90 days
  • AI governance framework + policy set (San José GovAI templates as the base — no blank pages)
  • Risk-tier every live PoC; human-in-the-loop rules per tier
  • Baseline 311 volumes and cost per contact for benefits realization
Months 3–6
  • Launch the Brampton Public AI Register — every AI system, its data, its oversight, in plain language
  • Trusted 311 pilot with grounded answers, audit logging, and human escalation
  • AI literacy program for people leaders begins
Months 6–12
  • Trusted 311 to full production, multilingual, 24/7
  • First public benefits-realization scorecard to Council
  • Scale playbook applied to next PoC in the priority stack

Step 5 · The National Layer

AI for All needs a municipal proving ground. Brampton is the obvious one.

Canada's national AI strategy (June 2026) sets six priorities — adoption from 12% to 60% of businesses by 2034, AI literacy at national scale, sovereign capability, and risk mitigation. Strategies need places where they become real. A city of 250 cultures and 115 languages, with a AAA credit rating and a live AI program, is where "for All" gets proven.

Adoption — already funded

Brampton's BHive hub carries a $2.7M federal Regional AI Initiative investment to drive AI adoption in local manufacturing, life sciences, and logistics SMEs. The City's AI program should interlock with it — one civic AI story, enterprise and economy together.

Literacy — inclusive by design

The national strategy targets AI literacy at massive scale. Brampton can pair workforce enablement with community AI literacy in Canada's most diverse big city — the version of the national target that actually reaches underrepresented communities.

Trust — the federal-municipal bridge

A municipal Public AI Register and governance framework operationalizes the strategy's risk-mitigation pillar locally, and aligns with Ontario's Bill 194 direction. Brampton becomes the reference implementation other Canadian cities copy.

Sport — the legacy multiplier

A $1B federal grassroots sport commitment, Canada Soccer's $9.8M legacy training-centre fund, a Prime Ministerial nod to Brampton's soccer facilities, and cricket's boom centred on the TD Cricket Arena. An AI-enabled venue, visitor, and fan-experience strategy makes Brampton's bids stronger and its sporting moment measurable.

Step 6 · The Hard Questions

Anticipated objections — answered before they're asked

Isn't this just another chatbot, like the one New York is shutting down?
No — it's the opposite sequence. NYC launched at scale in beta without grounding, risk-tiering, or a transparency mechanism, and paid for it. This blueprint puts the governance framework, the risk tiers, and the public register in place first, launches 311 on grounded answers with audit logging and human escalation, and reports measured results to Council before scaling anything else.
What about resident privacy and bias?
They're the spine of the program, not a checkbox. Every use case gets an impact assessment (the federal Algorithmic Impact Assessment is a ready-made instrument), bias evaluation appropriate to its risk tier, and a plain-language entry in the public register residents can read and challenge. Privacy governance in complex regulated environments is the work I've done for a decade.
Will AI eliminate City jobs?
The design goal is redirection, not reduction: containing routine contacts so staff time moves to complex cases, and equipping 6,000 employees with governed AI tools and literacy training. The benefits-realization scorecard reports staff-hours redirected — a number Council and unions can both scrutinize.
What does this cost, and how do we know it's working?
The flagship rides on procurement already underway in the PoC program; the governance framework is largely adaptable from free GovAI Coalition templates. The discipline that makes it defensible is benefits realization: baseline first, measure against it, publish the scorecard. If a use case can't show value, it says so publicly — that's the point.
Does this lock Brampton into one vendor?
No. The reference architecture is layered by design — data foundation, workflows, agents, interfaces — with the governance spine running across all four. Vendors within a layer stay swappable; the City owns the operating model and the register, not the other way around.
Why should Brampton go first on a public AI register?
Because someone in Canada will, and the first mover defines the standard. Helsinki and Amsterdam proved the model; Bill 194 shows Ontario's direction; Brampton already has the media scrutiny. A register converts that scrutiny from liability into leadership — at a fraction of the cost of one failed deployment.
